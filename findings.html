Findings: what each participant said

What participants said, kept separate from our interpretation, which is in M2. Short quotes are English translations of the Romanian transcript. Timestamps link to the transcript.

Gabi

  • Described evaluating news as something debated “at the office with my colleagues” that “happens daily”. Gabi did not describe a specific recent case. 00:12
  • Judges a story by its source: the person it comes from, whether they have “a good reputation or a bad reputation”, and how much Gabi trusts the newspaper or TV channel. 00:57, 01:36
  • Compares outlets: if several websites or channels present a story the same way, “it means it’s OK”; if they differ, “something doesn’t add up”. 02:43
  • Said people with strong doctrinal or political beliefs call contradicting information false, and that Gabi has “no reason to bother” trying to persuade them. 03:36, 04:12
  • Said AI-made stories are “hard to counter” because AI “knows how to formulate fairly plausible arguments”, calling them “a real danger”. 05:02
  • On the interviewer’s get-rich-quick example, Gabi pointed to its implausibility: overnight wealth, access limited to “the bank people”, and a sign-up website. Gabi also said “a fairly large percentage of the population” cannot tell false stories from true ones. 06:26
  • Asked about solutions, Gabi proposed civic education starting in school or kindergarten. For adults, Gabi proposed mass-media programmes, especially on public TV or through state partnerships with high-rated channels. Gabi did not mention an app or tool, including after the interviewer suggested “an app or a course”. 08:07, 09:43

Baldo

  • Most recent case: a fact concerning “a personal investment”. Baldo searched Google and checked “two or three websites” for “a credible source behind it”. 00:13, 00:32, 01:06
  • Found it convincing because an official institution published it. Baldo attributed the document to the “FCC” but was unsure what the abbreviation stands for. 01:24
  • Checking is time-consuming when a claim is not “binary”, because it needs several sources and opinions from various people. Binary claims are “much easier”. 02:20
  • Skipped investigating when a claim “wasn’t that important”. 03:21
  • Tools: community notes on X/Twitter, and asking “an AI agent” for sources “both for and against”. For very important claims Baldo checks each source in more detail; otherwise a simple search, “AI-assisted or manual”, may be enough. 03:41
  • Said “pretty much everyone” is vulnerable, because people are less likely to check something that fits what they already believe. Baldo gave a hypothetical political example. 05:48, 06:30
  • Said spotting misinformation has become harder in the AI era. Tells for fake images and videos stop working as generators improve; for text, finding sources is “a better sign … but it isn’t 100%”. Baldo has not personally encountered voice deepfakes. 07:22, 07:45

Georgiana

  • Most recent case, about a week before the interview: news about measures taken over strikes in France. Georgiana wanted to know whether the source was official, and checked official websites as well as news sites. 00:21, 00:37
  • It mattered because Georgiana lives there now and needed to know whether the measures applied to them. 01:04
  • The hard part was understanding: some information was incomplete, some wording was unfamiliar, and stories pointed to “a lot of references” through links instead of explaining the whole point. 01:31
  • Studies the energy sector and notices stories in that field that “aren’t written correctly in terms of the words they use”, as well as “far more exaggerated headlines to grab attention”. 02:24
  • Doesn’t look further when a subject isn’t of particular interest or when Georgiana already knows the correct information. 02:24
  • Tools: searches alone when familiar with the subject, relying on trusted sources such as technical publications and better-regarded news channels; for something more specific, asks people who might know the subject. 03:26
  • In general groups with colleagues, stories get forwarded, but Georgiana doesn’t think people were completely fooled: they mostly sent them to ask the others’ opinion. Georgiana has seen people who are fooled, though not in their close circle. 04:36
  • Said it is getting harder: generated images “can be used as false proof” and are of good quality, so publications need to check images more thoroughly. Responsibility also lies with people checking sources, and possibly with stricter legislation and penalties for fake news. 05:46

WhatsApp interview (P01, anonymous)

From the team’s summary of a WhatsApp conversation; no transcript is published. Read the summary

  • Came across a misleading claim that Birmingham was banning the English flag, questioned it, and checked it with a simple online search.
  • Their main frustration was seeing others believe the claim and being unable to convince a friend otherwise.
  • Often moves on from questionable claims when the topic feels unimportant or when distracted.
  • Prefers several independent sources that are not AI-generated.
  • On LogiQ: found it interesting and potentially helpful if implemented well, but suggested explaining its purpose more clearly and in a friendlier way.
