03 / OPTIONAL DEPTH

A label is a starting point, not the whole explanation.

WHEN THE DISTINCTION MATTERS

Potential appeal to popularity

Popularity can tell us what people believe. It becomes a reasoning problem when it is used as sufficient proof that a factual claim is true. The claim might still be true; this argument does not establish it.

In plain language: what is a reasoning flaw?

A gap between the reasons offered and the conclusion. A conclusion can still be true even when the argument given for it is weak.

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

What evidence supports the claim beyond the number of shares?

  • Find the original claim and read its surrounding context.
  • Look for evidence that directly supports the conclusion.
  • Compare independent sources, including relevant counterevidence.

These are suggestions, not retrieved sources. Missing evidence in this passage does not mean evidence does not exist.
