A CLAIM YOU CHOOSE TO EXAMINE

“Thousands of people shared this claim, so it must be true.”
QPopularity is not evidence.

Lots of shares tell us that a claim has spread. They do not tell us whether it is supported.

A QUESTION WORTH ASKING

What evidence supports the claim beyond the number of shares?

A weak argument does not necessarily mean a false claim.

Less “trust this verdict.” More “look at this reasoning.”

FROM A PASSAGE TO A BETTER QUESTION

Stay curious.
Keep the final say.

This is the experience we are exploring. Start with one passage, not a feed full of warnings.

01 / CHOOSE

Something feels like a leap.

Select a passage and ask for a second look. In the prototype, choose one of three sample arguments.

02 / UNDERSTAND

See why, in plain language.

Read a short explanation tied to the exact words. Open more context when you want the nuance.

03 / QUESTION

Find your next question.

Consider what evidence would help. Share an explanation, disagree with it, or return to reading.

A SECOND LOOK, NOT THE LAST WORD

An explanation.
Not a truth score.

LogiQ is meant to support your reasoning, not replace it. The distinction matters.

REASONING, NOT A VERDICT

Question the argument.

A reasoning flaw alone cannot tell you if a claim is true or false. This prototype does not verify facts or retrieve sources.

CONTEXT, NOT CERTAINTY

Leave room for nuance.

Read the caveats and challenge the explanation. Labels describe potential issues, not the author's intentions.

YOUR PACE, YOUR CHOICE

Close it and keep reading.

Deeper context, sharing and feedback are optional. No account is needed; the prototype sends no analysis or feedback data.
