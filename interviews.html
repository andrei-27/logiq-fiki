WhatsApp interview

Interview 01 — Social Media Misinformation

P01 · Anonymous social media user · 1 November 2026

Method: WhatsApp interview

Interview summary

The participant encountered a misleading claim that Birmingham was allegedly banning the English flag. They questioned it and checked through a simple online search.

The main frustration was seeing others believe the claim and being unable to convince a friend otherwise. They also said they often move on from questionable claims when a topic feels unimportant or they become distracted.

When checking information, they prefer multiple independent sources that are not AI-generated.

Product feedback

The participant found LogiQ interesting and potentially helpful if implemented well, but suggested explaining its purpose and use more clearly and in a friendlier way.

Key findings

  • Verifying a claim may be manageable; persuading someone else can be harder.
  • Low interest and distraction can interrupt fact-checking.
  • The participant values trust and independent verification.
  • LogiQ’s value proposition should be clearer and more approachable.

Impact on LogiQ

  1. Investigate a simpler, more intuitive interface and explanation.
  2. Explore explanations that are easy to understand and share in discussions about misleading arguments.

Conclusion

This interview suggests a possible need for explanations of misleading reasoning, rather than a tool that only labels information as false. Further interviews are needed to test whether this need is shared by a wider segment.

Evidence: an anonymized WhatsApp conversation; screenshots are retained privately.
