02 / THE PROBLEM & THE IDEA

More information.
Less room to reflect.

Online, a confident claim can feel like a convincing argument. They are not always the same thing.

Headlines, posts, and comment threads mix facts with emotional appeals, personal attacks, and unsupported conclusions. Checking a claim takes effort; understanding how it tries to persuade us takes another kind of attention.

Our starting hypothesis: short explanations, offered in context, can help people evaluate arguments without interrupting the way they read.

↳

The question we’re exploring
Can a small moment of reflection become a lasting critical-thinking habit?

OUR VALUE PROPOSITION

A second look.
Not the last word.

LogiQ is designed to explain what may be problematic in an argument, so the reader can decide what to think.

01 — NOTICE

Spot the reasoning.

Highlight a passage that may contain a logical flaw or a persuasive technique. Start with selected text; explore contextual browser support next.

02 — UNDERSTAND

Explain the leap.

Show a short, plain-language explanation tied to the exact words. Offer more detail only when the reader wants it.

03 — REFLECT

Ask a better question.

Suggest what evidence or context is missing. Invite the reader to challenge the analysis and give feedback.

THE ARGUMENT

“Thousands of people shared this claim, so it must be true.”
QPotential appeal to popularity

A claim’s popularity tells us how widely it has spread, not whether the evidence supports it. People can share something for many reasons.

ASK YOURSELF

What evidence supports the claim beyond the number of shares?

A little more context +

Popularity can tell us what people believe. It becomes a reasoning problem when it is used as sufficient proof that a factual claim is true. The claim might still be true; this argument does not establish it.

THE PRODUCT WE WANT TO BUILD

↗

Explain, don’t dictate.

Make the reasoning visible and leave the conclusion with the reader.

≈

Leave room for nuance.

Flag uncertainty. Strong emotion alone does not make an argument invalid.

◎

Earn the interruption.

Prefer a few useful insights over a feed full of warnings.

04 / WHO WE’RE BUILDING FOR

Start focused.
Learn with people.

Customer segments are hypotheses to test. Our proposed first audience is students and educators, where feedback and learning outcomes can be observed.

01
INITIAL FOCUS

Students & curious readers

People who read, debate, and want to understand why an argument works—or doesn’t.

Need: quick, concrete explanations while reading.
02
POTENTIAL BUYERS

Educators & media-literacy teams

Teachers and organizations looking for accessible examples and guided critical-thinking exercises.

Need: useful teaching material and observable learning.
03
LONGER-TERM EXPLORATION

Readers seeking more confidence

People who feel overwhelmed by online information and need an especially simple experience.

Need: clarity, trust, and low effort to get started.

OUR ADVANTAGE HYPOTHESIS

Useful explanations at the moment of reading can make critical thinking easier to practice. Clear language, contextual delivery, and progressive detail are our proposed differentiators.

06 / DEFINING SUCCESS

Measure understanding.
Not just attention.

We want to learn whether LogiQ helps people think more clearly. These are proposed measures; no live usage data has been collected for this SE project.

PROPOSED NORTH STAR

Weekly readers who find an explanation useful.

Count unique active readers who open an explanation and explicitly rate it as helpful during the week.

Activation
Share of new users who read their first explanation.
Learning
Change in performance on comparable before-and-after argument exercises.
Quality
False-positive rate on a reviewed evaluation set; reader disagreement tracked separately.
Retention & friction
Week-four return rate, dismissals, and extension disablement.
